Methodology

Causal Atlas is a structured catalog of datasets and data generators for causal research. It supports resource selection by describing the properties relevant to causal analysis, including study design, intervention regimes, observation structure and available evaluation references. The current catalog contains 100 records, of which 95 form the core collection and five are designated as adjacent resources. Each record is represented by a common schema of 34 properties and links to the original provider.

Resource identification

A broad, iterative search was conducted using the GPT-6 Astra model with web search and document retrieval, under researcher direction. Candidate resources were investigated through primary publications, provider documentation and source repositories. Search was followed by targeted retrieval to clarify dataset characteristics and resolve gaps in the annotations. The unit of inclusion was a dataset, generator or identifiable release, with named configurations retained where properties depended on the experimental setup. This separation of source discovery from synthesis is consistent with retrieval-grounded research workflows such as STORM [1]. The search sought broad coverage across causal research rather than an exhaustive systematic review.

Schema and annotation

The schema was developed and refined to permit comparison across measured, synthetic and semi-synthetic resources. Its use of explicit definitions and controlled values follows the general documentation principles of Datasheets for Datasets [2] and is closely related to schema-based metadata extraction in MOLE [3]. GPT-6 Astra mapped source information to these fields, retaining citations and evidence locations for individual annotations. Source-reported statements were distinguished from curator mappings. Configuration-dependent properties were recorded within their applicable scope. Evaluation references were described by object, basis, scope, coverage and visibility, preserving the distinction between a simulated reference graph and experimentally measured outcomes.

Evidence review

Initial annotation was followed by successive review passes addressing record boundaries, source support and consistency across the catalog. Targeted follow-up searches revisited unresolved properties, and a final review reassessed the records and remaining uncertainties. Claims that could not be established were retained with an explicit review state rather than assigned a guessed value. This evidence-attribution and abstention policy is consistent with the framework examined by Buchmann et al. [4]. Filters use only documented properties and require all selected criteria to hold within a single record scope or named configuration.
